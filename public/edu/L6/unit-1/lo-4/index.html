LO 4: Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis is a powerful tool in engineering for dealing with uncertainty and variability in data. Whether you are analyzing manufacturing tolerances, material properties, or experimental results, statistics help you make informed decisions based on data.

Central Tendency

Measures of central tendency describe the center of a data set. The most common measures are the mean (average), median (middle value), and mode (most frequent value).

Mean: The sum of all values divided by the total number of values.

x̄ = (∑x) / n

Example: The lengths of five steel rods are measured as 10.1cm, 10.2cm, 9.9cm, 10.0cm, and 10.1cm. The mean length is:

  • x̄ = (10.1 + 10.2 + 9.9 + 10.0 + 10.1) / 5 = 50.3 / 5 = 10.06cm

Standard Deviation

Standard deviation (σ) measures the amount of variation or dispersion in a set of values. A low standard deviation indicates that the values tend to be close to the mean, while a high standard deviation indicates that the values are spread out over a wider range. This is critical in quality control to ensure parts meet specified tolerances.

The formula for the sample standard deviation is:

s = √[ ∑(x - x̄)2 / (n - 1) ]

Where x represents each value, x̄ is the mean, and n is the number of values.

Normal Distribution

The normal distribution, or bell curve, is a continuous probability distribution that is symmetrical around its mean. Many natural phenomena and manufacturing processes follow a normal distribution.

In a normal distribution:

  • Approximately 68% of the data falls within one standard deviation of the mean (±1σ).
  • Approximately 95% falls within two standard deviations (±2σ).
  • Approximately 99.7% falls within three standard deviations (±3σ).

Application: If a machine produces bolts with a mean diameter of 10mm and a standard deviation of 0.1mm, we can expect that 95% of the bolts produced will have a diameter between 9.8mm and 10.2mm.
