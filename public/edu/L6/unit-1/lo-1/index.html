LO 1: Algebraic Methods

Algebra is the backbone of engineering analysis. In this learning outcome, you will review and master the techniques required to transpose complex formulas, solve simultaneous equations, and work with polynomials.

Transposing Formulas

In mechanical engineering, you often know the result of a formula but need to isolate a specific variable (like solving for the radius in a stress calculation). The golden rule is: whatever you do to one side of the equation, you must do to the other.

Example: Transposing a Stress Formula

Consider the formula for axial stress: σ = F / A

Where:

  • σ = Stress (N/m² or Pa)
  • F = Applied Force (N)
  • A = Cross-sectional Area (m²)

If we have a circular cross-section, the area A is given by A = πr². Substituting this into the stress formula gives:

σ = F / (πr²)

Suppose we need to find the required radius (r) to keep the stress below a certain material limit for a given force. We must transpose the formula to solve for r:

  1. Multiply both sides by r²:
    σ × r² = F / π
  2. Divide both sides by σ:
    r² = F / (π × σ)
  3. Take the square root of both sides:
    r = √(F / (π × σ))

This is a fundamental skill in design engineering, ensuring components are adequately sized.

Simultaneous Equations

When analyzing static structures (like trusses), you often have multiple unknown forces but multiple equilibrium equations (sum of forces in X, sum of forces in Y). Solving these requires simultaneous equations.

Example: Solving a 2D Static System

Imagine a point in equilibrium subjected to two unknown forces, F1 and F2. The equations of equilibrium in the x and y directions yield:

1) 3F1 + 2F2 = 120 (from ΣFx = 0)
2) F1 - 4F2 = -30 (from ΣFy = 0)

To find F1 and F2, we can use the elimination method:

  1. Multiply equation (2) by 3 so the coefficients of F1 match:
    3(F1 - 4F2) = 3(-30) → 3F1 - 12F2 = -90
  2. Subtract this new equation from equation (1):
    (3F1 + 2F2) - (3F1 - 12F2) = 120 - (-90)
    14F2 = 210
  3. Solve for F2:
    F2 = 210 / 14 = 15 N
  4. Substitute F2 = 15 back into equation (2):
    F1 - 4(15) = -30
    F1 - 60 = -30
    F1 = 30 N

The solution is F1 = 30 N and F2 = 15 N.
