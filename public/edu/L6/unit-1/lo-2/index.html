LO 2: Trigonometry

Trigonometry is essential in engineering for analyzing forces, resolving vectors, and understanding wave motions. At SCQF Level 6, you are expected to confidently use trigonometric ratios, the sine rule, and the cosine rule to solve complex geometric problems.

Right-Angled Triangles and SOH CAH TOA

For right-angled triangles, the relationships between the angles and the lengths of the sides are defined by the sine, cosine, and tangent ratios. A common mnemonic to remember these is SOH CAH TOA:

  • SOH: Sine(θ) = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • CAH: Cosine(θ) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • TOA: Tangent(θ) = Opposite / Adjacent

Example: In a roof truss, if a rafter is 5m long (Hypotenuse) and makes an angle of 30° with the horizontal tie beam, the height (Opposite) of the roof can be found using the sine ratio:

  • Sine(30°) = Height / 5m
  • Height = 5m × Sine(30°) = 5m × 0.5 = 2.5m

The Sine and Cosine Rules

When dealing with non-right-angled triangles, SOH CAH TOA cannot be directly applied. Instead, we use the Sine and Cosine rules. These are crucial for analyzing structures with arbitrary geometries.

The Sine Rule

The Sine Rule states that the ratio of the length of a side to the sine of its opposite angle is constant for all three sides of a triangle:

a / Sin(A) = b / Sin(B) = c / Sin(C)

Use the Sine Rule when you know either two angles and one side, or two sides and a non-included angle.

The Cosine Rule

The Cosine Rule relates the lengths of the sides of a triangle to the cosine of one of its angles:

a2 = b2 + c2 - 2bc × Cos(A)

Use the Cosine Rule when you know either three sides (to find an angle) or two sides and the included angle (to find the third side).
