LO 2: Trigonometry

Trigonometry is essential in engineering for dealing with angles, waves, and resolving vectors. In this learning outcome, you will explore how trigonometric functions are applied to solve practical engineering problems, such as resolving forces in structural analysis or analyzing alternating current (AC) waveforms in electrical engineering.

Resolving Force Vectors

Forces rarely act in purely horizontal or vertical directions. When a force acts at an angle, it can be split (resolved) into two perpendicular components, typically an x-component (horizontal) and a y-component (vertical). This makes it much easier to perform equilibrium calculations using basic algebra.

Example: Resolving an Angled Force

Suppose a cable is pulling a crate with a force of 500 N at an angle of 30° above the horizontal.

To find the horizontal (Fx) and vertical (Fy) components, we use sine and cosine functions from basic right-angled triangle geometry (SOH CAH TOA):

  • cos(θ) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse → cos(30°) = Fx / 500
  • sin(θ) = Opposite / Hypotenuse → sin(30°) = Fy / 500

Rearranging to solve for the components:

  1. Fx = 500 × cos(30°)
    Fx ≈ 500 × 0.866 = 433 N
  2. Fy = 500 × sin(30°)
    Fy = 500 × 0.5 = 250 N

This means the cable provides a forward horizontal pull of 433 N and an upward vertical lift of 250 N.

Sine and Cosine Rules

Not all triangles in engineering are right-angled. For non-right-angled triangles (scalene or oblique), we use the Sine Rule and the Cosine Rule to find missing side lengths or angles. These are particularly useful in surveying or when adding non-perpendicular vectors graphically (e.g., in a vector polygon).

  • Sine Rule: a / sin(A) = b / sin(B) = c / sin(C)
  • Cosine Rule: a² = b² + c² - 2bc × cos(A)
