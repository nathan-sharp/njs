LO 1: Fluid Properties

Understanding the properties of fluids (liquids and gases) is the first step in analyzing thermofluid systems. In this learning outcome, you will explore key properties such as density, specific volume, specific gravity, and viscosity, and learn how to apply them in engineering calculations.

Density and Specific Volume

Density (ρ) is defined as mass per unit volume. The SI unit for density is kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m3).

ρ = m / V

Specific volume (v) is the reciprocal of density. It is defined as volume per unit mass, with SI units of cubic meters per kilogram (m3/kg).

v = V / m = 1 / ρ

Example: A tank contains 500 kg of a fluid and has a volume of 0.6 m3. Calculate the density and specific volume of the fluid.

  • ρ = m / V = 500 kg / 0.6 m3 = 833.33 kg/m3
  • v = 1 / ρ = 1 / 833.33 kg/m3 = 0.0012 m3/kg

Specific Gravity (Relative Density)

Specific gravity (SG), or relative density, is the ratio of the density of a fluid to the density of a reference fluid (usually water at 4°C for liquids, where ρwater ≈ 1000 kg/m3). Since it is a ratio of densities, it is a dimensionless quantity.

SG = ρfluid / ρreference

Example: If a certain oil has a specific gravity of 0.85, calculate its density.

  • SG = ρoil / ρwater
  • ρoil = SG × ρwater = 0.85 × 1000 kg/m3 = 850 kg/m3
