LO 2: Thermodynamics Fundamentals

Thermodynamics is the study of energy, heat, and work, and how they relate to the properties of matter. In this learning outcome, you will cover the foundational concepts of thermodynamics, focusing on the First Law of Thermodynamics and energy transfer processes.

The First Law of Thermodynamics

The First Law of Thermodynamics, also known as the Law of Conservation of Energy, states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another. For a closed system undergoing a cycle, the net heat transfer is equal to the net work done.

For a process from state 1 to state 2, the equation is:

Q - W = ΔU

Where:

  • Q is the heat transferred to the system.
  • W is the work done by the system.
  • ΔU is the change in internal energy of the system.

Example: A closed cylinder contains a gas. The gas is heated, adding 500 J of heat energy to it. The gas expands, doing 200 J of work on a piston. Calculate the change in internal energy of the gas.

  • Q = 500 J
  • W = 200 J
  • ΔU = Q - W = 500 J - 200 J = 300 J

The internal energy of the gas has increased by 300 J.

Sensible and Latent Heat

Heat transfer can result in a change of temperature (sensible heat) or a change of phase without a change of temperature (latent heat).

Sensible Heat Transfer: Q = mcΔT

Latent Heat Transfer: Q = mL

Example: How much heat is required to raise the temperature of 2 kg of water from 20°C to 80°C? (Specific heat capacity of water, c = 4.18 kJ/kgċK)

  • m = 2 kg
  • c = 4.18 kJ/kgċK
  • ΔT = 80°C - 20°C = 60°C = 60 K
  • Q = mcΔT = 2 × 4.18 × 60 = 501.6 kJ
