Algorithm Theory & Dithering Guide

Error Diffusion (Floyd-Steinberg, Atkinson, JJN): Quantizing continuous grayscale pixels ($0\dots255$) down to pure 1-bit binary (black or white) introduces severe quantization error. Error diffusion algorithms calculate this remainder ($e = \text{old\_pixel} - \text{new\_pixel}$) and distribute fractions of $e$ to neighboring unvisited pixels ahead of the scanline. Atkinson dithering (classic early Macintosh style) distributes only $3/4$ of the error, causing highlights and shadows to blow out with crisp, distinct contrast.

Ordered Dithering (Bayer 2x2, 4x4, 8x8): Unlike error diffusion which processes pixels sequentially, ordered dithering compares each pixel against a fixed threshold matrix ($M_{n\times n}$). Because every pixel is calculated independently of its neighbors, Bayer dithering is extremely fast and produces stable, non-flickering patterns—making it ideal for real-time video feeds.

Lo-Fi Audio PCM Downsampling: Reducing audio bit depth to 8-bit, 4-bit, or 2-bit introduces characteristic quantization noise and bit-crushed harmonic distortion typical of retro game consoles and vintage digital samplers.
