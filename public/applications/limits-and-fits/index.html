Understanding the ISO 286 System of Limits & Fits

ISO 286 defines the internationally standardized system of linear dimensional tolerances and engineering fits for cylindrical and parallel mating features. Each tolerance class combines a Fundamental Deviation letter (uppercase $\text{A}\dots\text{ZC}$ for holes, lowercase $\text{a}\dots\text{zc}$ for shafts) with an International Tolerance Grade (IT) number.

$$i = 0.45\sqrt[3]{D} + 0.001D \quad (\mu\text{m})$$

Where $i$ is the standard tolerance factor in micrometers ($\mu\text{m}$) and $D = \sqrt{D_{\min} \cdot D_{\max}}$ is the geometric mean of the nominal size step in millimeters. From the upper and lower deviations ($ES, EI$ for the hole and $es, ei$ for the shaft), the drawing limits and assembly clearances are calculated as:

$$D_{\max} = D + \frac{ES}{1000}, \qquad D_{\min} = D + \frac{EI}{1000}, \qquad D_{\text{mean}} = \frac{D_{\max} + D_{\min}}{2} \pm \frac{T_D}{2000}$$ $$d_{\max} = D + \frac{es}{1000}, \qquad d_{\min} = D + \frac{ei}{1000}, \qquad d_{\text{mean}} = \frac{d_{\max} + d_{\min}}{2} \pm \frac{T_d}{2000}$$ $$C_{\max} = ES - ei = D_{\max} - d_{\min}, \qquad C_{\min} = EI - es = D_{\min} - d_{\max}$$
